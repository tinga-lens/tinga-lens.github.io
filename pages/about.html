About Tinga Lens

Independent maps of Ghana's land, water and weather, district by district.

Satellites record a great deal about Ghana every day, but most of it sits in files that are hard to open and harder to read. Tinga Lens takes those public records and turns them into plain maps and figures for each district: how dry it is, where fires are burning, how green the land is, how much forest has gone and how fast towns are growing.

It is meant for people who need to know what is happening on the land and do not have a remote sensing team: farmers' groups, district officers, journalists, students and researchers. "Tinga" means land in Gurune, a language of the Upper East Region.

What it is not

Tinga Lens is not a warning service. For forecasts, warnings and emergencies, go to the Ghana Meteorological Agency and the National Disaster Management Organisation.

Independence

Tinga Lens is an independent project. It is not affiliated with any university, company or government agency.

Everything here comes from public datasets and from code written for this project. No university research code, unpublished models or restricted data are used.

How to cite

To cite Tinga Lens as a whole:

Adekilae, F. A. (2026). Tinga Lens: Environmental monitoring for Ghana. https://tingalens.org. Accessed [date].

To cite one map or figure, use the "Cite this map" text beside the map. It names the map, its period and version, the date you viewed it, and the original data source, for example:

Adekilae, F. A. (2026). Rainfall compared with normal: Jun to Aug 2026, against 1991–2020. Tinga Lens, product version 1.1. https://tingalens.org/pages/drought.html#drought. Accessed 3 October 2026. Source data: CHIRPS v3.0.

Please also credit the original data provider named on each map. The maps change as new data arrive, so always give the date you viewed them.

Using the data

You may use the figures. Please credit Tinga Lens and the original data source named on each map. See Data & methods for sources, limits and versions.
