Observed and condition products

The rainfall, soil moisture, vegetation, fire detection and tree cover products come from published datasets that their producers have validated. Tinga Lens has not yet carried out its own systematic comparison against ground measurements in Ghana. Until it does, treat the maps as guides. Reports from the ground that agree or disagree with a map are welcome: see the About page.

How to cite

To cite Tinga Lens as a whole:

Adekilae, F. A. (2026). Tinga Lens: Environmental monitoring for Ghana. https://tingalens.org. Accessed [date].

To cite one map or figure, use the "Cite this map" text beside the map. It names the map, its period and version, the date you viewed it, and the original data source, for example:

Adekilae, F. A. (2026). Rainfall compared with normal: Jun to Aug 2026, against 1991–2020. Tinga Lens, product version 1.1. https://tingalens.org/pages/drought.html#drought. Accessed 3 October 2026. Source data: CHIRPS v3.0.

Please also credit the original data provider named on each map. The maps change as new data arrive, so always give the date you viewed them.

Precision, baselines and status of the results

Resolution. Most products average satellite data over each district. The resolution in the Products table (for example 1 km or 5 km) is the size of the source grid cells, not a claim of accuracy at that scale. A district value is an average over a large area and says little about a single field or village. Where a source is finer, for example 30 m soil maps or 10 m land cover, it is aggregated before it is shown, and the finer detail is not published.

Baselines. Each product that is compared with normal names its baseline period on its own map and in the Products table (for example rainfall against 1991–2020). Compare a map only with the baseline shown for it.

Experimental estimates. Fire-risk, flood-prone and similar classes are experimental estimates built from public data. They are not official forecasts or warnings. For warnings, follow the Ghana Meteorological Agency, the National Disaster Management Organisation and the Hydrological Authority.

Encoding. Some map images are stored as small 8-bit files, so a value is held in one of 255 steps between a low and a high value (the 2nd and 98th percentiles) written beside the file. Extreme values beyond that range are shown as the end of the scale, and values are rounded to the nearest step.

Failed updates. If a weekly update cannot reach a data source, the previous published version of that product stays online and its "last updated" date does not change.

Species records. Positions in downloads are rounded to 0.1 degree (about 11 km) so that exact sites of sensitive species are not published. Conservation status is not shown.

How the data are processed

Every Monday, programs in the public code repository download the newest data from each source, average it over each district, compare it with the record, and publish the results as small files that these pages read. The processing code is written for this project and uses only publicly available datasets.

Downloads are available as CSV from every map and district profile. Each file names its source, date and product version.
